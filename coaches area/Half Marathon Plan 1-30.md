# Half Marathon Plan 1:30

## Purpose

This is the **master training plan** for the goal of running a **1:30:00 half marathon**.

Every future training session should be planned against this document.  
The purpose of each session should be traceable to one or more of the performance requirements below.

---

## Primary Goal

**Target race:** 21 March 2027  
**Target time:** 1:30:00  
**Target average pace:** approximately **4:16/km**

The objective is not simply to practise running at 4:16/km.

The objective is to develop enough:

- aerobic capacity,
- lactate-threshold speed,
- 5K speed,
- 10K strength,
- running economy,
- durability,
- and half-marathon-specific endurance

that **4:16/km becomes sustainable for 21.1 km**.

---

## Key Benchmark Race

**Cotswold Airport Half Marathon**  
**Date:** 20 February 2027

This is the major pre-target benchmark.

It should be used to assess:

- current half-marathon fitness,
- pace durability,
- heart-rate response,
- late-race cardiac drift,
- fuelling,
- pacing execution,
- footwear,
- race preparation,
- and how close 1:30 fitness is before the final March block.

It does not automatically need to be an all-out 1:30 attempt.  
Its role is to provide the strongest race-specific evidence before the final peak.

# Performance Milestones

These are formal milestones in the plan.

A 1:30 half marathon requires an average pace equivalent to about **21:20 for 5K**, but the preferred standalone 5K performance is faster than this so that 4:16/km is not too close to maximum 5K effort.

The preferred standalone 10K target is faster than this to provide sufficient speed reserve.

# Physiological Baseline

Measured lactate-test anchors:

## LT1

- **Heart rate:** ~144 bpm
- **Pace:** ~6:34/km

## LT2

- **Heart rate:** ~165 bpm
- **Pace:** ~5:17/km

These values are starting physiological anchors, not permanent pace limits.

The key adaptation we want is:

> **Similar physiological cost → faster pace**

The pace associated with LT1 and LT2 should improve as fitness develops.

---

# Core Metrics We Will Track

Every meaningful training block should be assessed using these metrics.

## 1. Aerobic Economy

Track pace achieved at a comparable easy/aerobic heart rate.

Desired direction:

**same HR → faster pace**

Example progression:

6:10/km → 6:00/km → 5:50/km at a similar aerobic HR.

---

## 2. Threshold Development

Track pace achieved around threshold physiological effort.

Historical anchor:

**~5:17/km at ~165 bpm**

We want this pace to move progressively faster.

Do not force an arbitrary threshold pace if HR, breathing and RPE indicate that the physiological cost is wrong.

---

## 3. 5K Performance

Use selected parkruns or dedicated 5K tests to measure speed development.

Not every Saturday parkrun should be an all-out PB attempt.

---

## 4. 10K Performance

Use dedicated 10K tests/races periodically.

This is one of the most important indicators of whether the 1:30 target is becoming realistic.

---

## 5. Half-Marathon Pace Durability

Track how much controlled work can be completed close to target HM pace.

Later examples may include:

- 3 × 3 km
- 3 × 4 km
- 2 × 5 km
- long progressive runs
- continuous race-specific blocks

These should only be introduced when the underlying fitness supports them.

---

## 6. Long-Run Durability

Monitor:

- HR drift,
- pace fade,
- cadence stability,
- perceived effort,
- fuelling tolerance,
- muscular fatigue,
- and recovery after the run.

---

# Training Phases

## Phase 1 — Aerobic Rebuild and Strength

**Approximate period:** October–November 2026

Main objectives:

- rebuild consistent aerobic volume,
- recover fully from the September half marathon,
- improve pace below LT1,
- improve running economy,
- build lower-body strength,
- retain speed through strides and controlled parkrun work,
- begin moving threshold pace forward.

Priority:

**Consistency before intensity.**

---

## Phase 2 — Threshold and 10K Development

**Approximate period:** December 2026–January 2027

Main objectives:

- materially improve LT2 pace,
- move toward sub-22 5K,
- move toward sub-44 10K,
- increase sustainable threshold duration,
- improve ability to run faster without excessive HR rise,
- progressively increase weekly running volume if recovery supports it.

This is where running threshold work becomes increasingly important.

---

## Phase 3 — Half-Marathon Specific Development

**Approximate period:** late January–February 2027

Main objectives:

- convert improved 5K/10K fitness into HM durability,
- introduce longer blocks near HM pace,
- extend long runs,
- practise race fuelling,
- practise race shoes,
- develop control around 4:20–4:30/km before progressively approaching 4:16/km.

The goal is not to force 4:16/km too early.

---

## Phase 4 — Cotswold Airport Benchmark

**Race:** 20 February 2027

Use the result to determine:

- current race fitness,
- whether target pace is metabolically sustainable,
- where HR sits over 21.1 km,
- whether pace can be maintained late in the race,
- whether the final block should prioritise endurance, threshold, speed reserve, or recovery.

---

## Phase 5 — Final Specific Peak

**Approximate period:** late February–early March 2027

Main objectives:

- consolidate HM-specific fitness,
- complete final race-pace sessions,
- avoid unnecessary fatigue,
- sharpen rather than build.

---

## Phase 6 — Taper

**Final 10–14 days**

Main objectives:

- reduce fatigue,
- retain race-specific intensity,
- preserve neuromuscular sharpness,
- arrive fresh.

Fitness should already be built.

---

# Weekly Training Framework

This is the default structure. It can be modified when fatigue, races, illness, family commitments, recovery or performance data require it.

## Monday — Long Aerobic Run

Primary purpose:

- aerobic development,
- durability,
- fatigue resistance.

Early phases:

- mostly easy aerobic running.

Later phases:

- progressive finishes,
- steady sections,
- controlled HM-specific blocks.

Heart rate and session purpose take priority over arbitrary pace.

---

## Tuesday — Rest / Strength

Primary purpose:

- recovery,
- strength,
- injury resistance.

Key strength areas:

- Bulgarian split squats,
- Romanian deadlifts,
- calf raises,
- glute work,
- core,
- single-leg stability.

Strength should support running, not compromise key running sessions.

---

## Wednesday — Threshold / Aerobic Power

Primary purpose:

- improve LT2,
- improve sustainable speed,
- increase time at controlled hard effort.

Example progression:

- 4 × 8 min
- 3 × 10 min
- 3 × 12 min
- 2 × 15 min
- 2 × 20 min
- continuous threshold work

Wattbike may be used when cardiovascular load is useful but additional running impact is not.

---

## Thursday — Easy Aerobic + Strides

Primary purpose:

- aerobic volume,
- recovery,
- running economy,
- neuromuscular speed.

Typical structure:

- easy aerobic run,
- 4–6 × 20 second strides.

Do not turn this into another hard session.

---

## Friday — Rest / Very Easy

Used to absorb training and prepare for Saturday quality work.

---

## Saturday — Parkrun / 5K / Speed Development

Possible purposes:

- all-out 5K benchmark,
- controlled 5K,
- tempo session,
- HM-pace block,
- progression run,
- speed session.

Not every week is a PB attempt.

The purpose must be explicitly defined before the run.

---

## Sunday — Recovery

Primary purpose:

- absorb Saturday quality work,
- maintain movement,
- prepare for Monday long run.

Initially this may be:

- walking,
- very easy cycling,
- complete rest,
- very easy recovery running.

A second aerobic run can be added later if recovery and training volume justify it.

---

# Training Intensity Rules

## Easy and Long Running

The purpose of easy running is aerobic development, not pace validation.

Use:

**session purpose → HR/RPE → duration/distance → pace**

Do not force pace when the physiological cost is too high.

---

## Threshold Running

Judge threshold sessions using:

1. HR response
2. breathing/control
3. RPE
4. pace achieved

Pace is an outcome and performance marker.

---

## Race-Specific Work

Only once the underlying fitness is ready does pace become a primary prescription.

Target HM pace:

**~4:16/km**

The amount of work near this pace should increase gradually.

---

# Progression Gates

Training should progress because the athlete demonstrates readiness, not simply because the calendar moves forward.

## Gate 1 — Aerobic Improvement

Evidence:

- faster pace at similar easy HR,
- lower HR at previously familiar pace,
- reduced cardiac drift,
- good recovery.

---

## Gate 2 — Sub-23 5K

Indicates the first meaningful improvement in speed reserve.

---

## Gate 3 — Sub-46 10K

Indicates improved endurance at faster speeds.

---

## Gate 4 — Sub-22 5K

Signals that the speed gap to the target is closing materially.

---

## Gate 5 — Sub-44 10K

Important threshold before committing heavily to 1:30-specific HM work.

---

## Gate 6 — 5K Goal Range

**20:30–21:00**

Indicates strong enough speed reserve for a serious 1:30 attempt.

---

## Gate 7 — 10K Goal Range

**41:30–42:15**

One of the strongest pre-race indicators that 4:16/km half-marathon pace may be supportable.

---

## Gate 8 — HM-Specific Durability

Ability to complete substantial race-specific work close to HM target pace while remaining controlled.

---

# Session Planning Rule

Before adding any session to the calendar, answer:

1. **What adaptation is this session targeting?**
2. **Which milestone does it support?**
3. **Why is it appropriate now?**
4. **What is the correct intensity?**
5. **How does it interact with the previous and following sessions?**
6. **What metric will tell us whether it worked?**
7. **Does it create unnecessary fatigue that could compromise a more important session?**

If those questions cannot be answered, the session should not be added simply to fill the calendar.

---

# Review Process

After key sessions we should review:

- pace,
- HR,
- pace-at-HR,
- cardiac drift,
- cadence,
- elevation,
- split consistency,
- RPE,
- recovery,
- soreness,
- sleep/fatigue where relevant.

The master plan should be updated when the evidence shows that current training targets are no longer appropriate.

---

# Plan Principle

The goal is not to prove that Paul can briefly run at **4:16/km**.

The goal is to systematically build the physiology and durability that allow him to run:

> **4:16/km for 21.1 km without the effort becoming unsustainable.**

Every session should contribute to that outcome.
