# Coaching Knowledge Base for Paul's Run Debriefs

Compiled 2026-09-30 from web research. Sources are linked; claims are paraphrased. Treat blog sources as secondary and check the primary papers before leaning on a number.

## How to use this file
Read before a debrief. Ask questions first, interpret second. Compare what Paul felt with what the data shows, and treat mismatches as the useful signal, not as errors.

## 1. Principles from elite coaching

**Intensity distribution (Seiler and colleagues)**
- Elite endurance athletes across sports converge on mostly low-intensity training with a smaller block of hard work and little time in the middle. Roughly 80% of sessions low, about 20% high is the common summary; by time in heart-rate zone it can be closer to 90% low.
- A 2013 cyclist study found a polarized split (80/0/20) beat a threshold-heavy split (57/43/0) over six weeks. A 2025 systematic review of 11 studies put the average at about 81% low, 3% moderate, 15% high, and linked it to better running economy.
- Caveat: one review concluded an optimal distribution cannot be identified yet. Polarized is well supported, not proven best for everyone.
- A 2025 consensus commentary co-authored by Seiler defines Zone 2 as just below the first lactate threshold (about 1-2 mmol/L, roughly 70-80% of max HR).
- Practical takeaway for amateurs: keep easy days genuinely easy so the hard days can be genuinely hard.

**Norwegian model (threshold-dense)**
- Emphasises lots of controlled threshold work, often lactate-guided, sometimes two threshold sessions in one day, all sitting on top of a large volume of easy running.
- A 2024 Sports Medicine study interviewed 12 successful Norwegian coaches. Half used double-threshold days. Its key idea: elite coaches manage training stress and recovery, not just intensity, and success is framed as staying healthy enough to keep doing the work.
- A 2026 Frontiers review contrasts it with the East African model (fartlek, progressive runs, group training). Both produce world-class runners, so there is no single best system.
- TrainingPeaks (2026) argues the transferable principle is more controlled threshold work, not the double days or lactate testing themselves.

**Jack Daniels (died 12 Sep 2025)**
- Five training intensities (easy, marathon, threshold, interval, repetition) set from race-derived VDOT. Most mileage at easy pace.
- Good for simple pace setting from recent races. Criticised for not accounting for individual and day-to-day variation, which is exactly what a debrief is for.

**Renato Canova**
- Coach of 50+ Olympic or World Championship medallists. Known for specific-block and strength-endurance ideas. I found mostly secondary summaries; read his own material before quoting details.

## 2. Monitoring and subjective data
- Session RPE (Foster method) = RPE on a 1-10 scale multiplied by session minutes. Collected about 30 minutes after the session.
- A 2025 outdoor-running study found sRPE distinguished session types (endurance, submaximal, intervals) better than heart-rate-based TRIMP or biomechanical load. The authors recommend using several measures together and including subjective load.
- Athletes tend to report easier sessions as harder than coaches intended.
- One coach's practice: attach an expected perceived exertion to every session; when the reported score differs, investigate why (intensity, recovery, outside stress) and adjust together.
- Well-being questionnaires (Hooper's index: fatigue, stress, soreness, sleep) correlate modestly with RPE in trail runners.

## 3. Debrief question bank (my synthesis of the sources above)
**Open first, numbers second**
- How did that feel, and why do you think it felt that way?
- What went well? What would you change? What did you learn?

**Effort and expectation**
- What RPE (1-10) would you give it? What was it supposed to be?
- If they differ: what do you think explains the gap?

**Body**
- How did your legs feel in the first 5-10 minutes versus the end?
- How did your breathing feel as the effort built? Any moment it felt out of proportion to the pace?
- Any niggles? Did they change during the session?

**Context**
- Sleep, food, stress, time since last run, kit problems?
- Was the pace or effort what you intended? Who decided to change it, and when?

**Mindset**
- How was your confidence going in, and how is it now?
- Was there a point you wanted to stop? What was going on then?

**Looking forward**
- What would you like to do differently next time?
- Is there anything you're curious about that we could test?
